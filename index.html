From Rain to Groundwater

Discover how water travels beneath our feet and returns to the surface.

Groundwater forms when rainwater soaks into the soil, trickles down through gaps between soil, sand and rock, and fills the underground layers called aquifers. From there it slowly moves until it reaches a well, a spring or a river.

Real-Life Examples

🪣

Wells

A well is a hole drilled or dug below the water table. Groundwater seeps into it from surrounding pores and cracks, and a pump or bucket lifts it up.

⛲

Springs

Where the water table meets the land surface, such as on a hillside, groundwater flows out naturally as a spring.

🏞️

Rivers

Groundwater seeps into river beds and keeps rivers flowing during dry weeks. This steady supply is called base flow.

🌾

Agriculture

Farmers pump groundwater to irrigate crops when rain is unreliable, which makes it a major user of the resource.

🚰

Drinking Water

Groundwater is filtered naturally as it moves through soil and rock, so it is a key freshwater source for billions of people.

Quick Facts

🌍Groundwater is stored beneath the Earth's surface.
⏳Aquifers can contain water for long periods.
🌧️Rainfall is an important source of groundwater recharge.
⚙️Excessive pumping can lower the water table.
🏠Rainwater harvesting can help recharge groundwater.
